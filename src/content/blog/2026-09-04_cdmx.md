La Ciudad de México (CDMX) is my favorite city and is easily the most _interesting_ in North America.

It's like a mix of all my favorite things:
It has vibrant streets that rival Southeast Asia, colonial architecture that brings a European feel, adjacency and ambition from the US, while retaining prehispanic traditions [^history]

<!-- It retains Aztec traditions, colonial architecture and its pedestrian-oriented city design with small businesses to discover on every street, that is delightful to walk in, and yet it isn't so different from the US that it feels like entering some parallel universe. [^history] -->

I've been in and around it five times in the past few years, to visit and revisit places and neighborhoods. So these are my recommendations and ratings.

## Where to stay

**Stay in Roma Norte or in Condesa.** Roma Norte is more central so you'll be a short distance to a lot of the best restaurants, bars, street food, and clubs. But it's also loud at night. Condesa is more quiet and quaint, partly because it has many expats.

If you speak Spanish or have already been a few times, consider Santa María la Ribera or cheap options in the residential neighborhoods further south.

### Neighborhoods

![Map](/assets/cdmx-neighborhoods.png)

You can also take a look the meme-y but kinda accurate [Hoodmaps](https://hoodmaps.com/mexico-city) too.

In order of visit importance:

1. **Roma Norte** is the coolest all-around neighborhood with lots of food, bars, and vendors.
2. **Centro Histórico** has old colonial buildings and some upscale restaurants.
3. **Juárez** as La Zona Rosa as a gay neighborhood with extra nightlife.
4. **Condesa** doesn't have as much food but has the most beautiful parks and strolls.
5. **Coyoacán** is a cool historic area famous for Frida Kahlo but also has some nice parks and cobblestone roads.
6. **Polanco** has luxury shopping but is worth visiting its museum.
7. **Santa María la Ribera** is a pretty neighborhood in a rougher (authentic?) way yet also feels on the verge of gentrification. Cool time to visit!
8. **Reforma** is somehow a dead zone, not very walkable and not a lot going on.
9. **Tepito** and **Doctores** are more dangerous, but have some markets worth visiting especially with a local guide.
10. **Roma Sur**, **Escandón**, **Napoles**, and **Navarte** are more residential but can be worth seeing for even more, extra local food options.

## When to visit

The best time is **October to mid November.** It has temperate weather after the rainy season, better air quality, and includes Día de Muertos which brings cempasúchil flowers all over the city, bakeries selling "pan de muerto", and the huge parade.[^desfile]

## How to get around

**Use Uber and Didi.**

Don't be afraid to take the Metrobús but you'll need a metro card which you can buy at most of the stations from a vending machine.

Do be a little afraid of the underground Metro, but it can be useful, especially to Coyoacán, just be extra aware of your phone and stuff.
