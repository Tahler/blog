La Ciudad de México (CDMX) is my favorite city.

And easily North America's most _interesting_. It rivals New York in size and Southeast Asia in vibrant streets. It mixes some US-adjacent culture and ambition with colonial-era architecture with prehispanic traditions[^history]. from it was built on top of Lake Texcoco! The mix is reflected in its food, its art, and its urbanism.

I've been five times in the past few years to visit and revisit places and neighborhoods. Here are my recommendations.

## Where to stay

**Roma Norte or La Condesa.** Roma Norte is more central so you'll be a short distance to a lot of the best restaurants, bars, street food, and clubs. But almost every corner is loud at night. If you prefer quaint and quiet, La Condesa is only a bit farther. Both are very safe and beautiful.

If you speak Spanish or have already been a couple times, consider Santa María la Ribera or going for cheaper options in the residential neighborhoods further south.

### Neighborhoods

![Map](/assets/cdmx-neighborhoods.png)

Quick overview, in order of visit importance:

1. [**Roma Norte**](#roma-norte) is the coolest all-around neighborhood with lots of shops, food, cafes, bars, and clubs.
2. [**Centro Histórico**](#centro-histórico) has historic colonial buildings and some upscale restaurants.
3. [**La Condesa**](#la-condesa) isn't as bustling but has the most beautiful parks and strolls.
4. [**Coyoacán**](#coyoacán) is a charming area most famous for Frida Kahlo but it also has nice colonial buildings, parks, and cobblestone roads.
5. [**Juárez**](#juárez) is like an extension of Roma Norte but more modern, more gay, more nightlife.
6. [**Polanco**](#polanco) has luxury shopping and museums.
7. [**Santa María la Ribera**](#santa-maría-la-ribera) is a pretty neighborhood in a rougher way. Feels on the verge of gentrification.
8. [**Reforma**](#reforma) is worth walking (or biking on Sundays!) along the main road but also weirdly dead around the neighboring blocks.
9. [**Tepito**](#tepito) and **Doctores** are more dangerous, but also have the best markets.
10. [**Roma Sur, Escandón, Napoles, and Navarte**](#roma-sur-escandón-napoles-and-navarte) are more residential but that can be worth seeing, especially for extra-local food options.

You can also take a look the meme-y but kinda accurate [Hoodmaps](https://hoodmaps.com/mexico-city) too.

## When to visit

The best time is **October to mid November.** It has temperate weather after the rainy season, better air quality, and includes Día de Muertos which brings cempasúchil flowers all over the city, bakeries selling "pan de muerto", and the huge parade.[^desfile]

## How to get around

**Use Uber and Didi.**

Don't be afraid to take the Metrobús but you'll need a metro card which you can buy at most of the stations from a vending machine.

Do be a little afraid of the underground Metro, but it can be useful, especially to Coyoacán, just be extra aware of your phone and stuff.

🏛️ [Letras de Tepito](https://www.google.com/maps?cid=9634596570507595755)\
Honestly dope letters as far as Instagram-worthy place names go. Start/end of the tianguis

🛍️ [Tianguis La Lagunilla](https://www.google.com/maps?cid=1003923946432277311)\
Super worth a visit on Sundays ~11am-2pm, tons of street vendors and food and art. Walked from Lagunilla to las Letras. Tepito is infamously dangerous but it feels very safe at this time, just stay aware.

#### Chapultepec

🏛️ [Museo Nacional de Antropología](https://www.google.com/maps?cid=9521483601320789284)\
Enormous museum with lots of cool archaeological bones and drawings and textiles. Only some parts had English though, and my Spanish is barely literary 😬

🏛️ [Chapultepec Castle](https://www.google.com/maps?cid=12024611576922344242)\
It's cool! I didn't love love it, but I had high expectations and I'm comparing it to some European castles. Nonetheless, beautiful views of Chapultepec and Reforma.

🏛️ [Basilica of Our Lady of Guadalupe](https://www.google.com/maps?cid=2423194957463972528)\
